All Seven Spartamax Ingredients

The seller describes the formula as six botanical extracts plus L-arginine HCl. Per-gummy amounts are not published on the sales page, so check the Supplement Facts panel on your bottle.

L-Arginine HCl

An amino acid the seller links to drive and everyday energy.

Research context: Your body uses arginine to make nitric oxide, a molecule involved in blood-vessel relaxation. NIH reviews describe mixed results for exercise performance.

Tongkat Ali

A Southeast Asian root the seller includes for male vitality support.

Research context: Also called Eurycoma longifolia or “long jack,” it has a long traditional history. Human evidence is still limited, and NIH LiverTox notes rare reports of liver injury.

Maca Root

A Peruvian root the seller associates with natural energy.

Research context: Maca has been eaten as a food for centuries in the Andes. MedlinePlus describes it as likely safe in food amounts and notes only limited research on its other uses.

Ashwagandha

An adaptogenic botanical the seller says promotes healthy hormone function.

Research context: This herb is used in Ayurvedic tradition. NCCIH summarizes small studies on stress and sleep and lists safety precautions.

Horny Goat Weed

A traditional botanical tonic the seller includes for daily stamina.

Research context: This is the Epimedium plant. MedlinePlus says there is not enough reliable information yet to say whether it helps, and it advises avoiding it during pregnancy.

Beet Root

A nitrate-rich root the seller links to circulation and to signals between brain and body.

Research context: Dietary nitrate from beets converts to nitric oxide. Meta-analyses report modest effects on exercise endurance in healthy adults.

Grape Seed

An antioxidant the seller links to blood-vessel health and endurance.

Research context: Grape seed extract contains proanthocyanidins. NCCIH reviews research on blood pressure and circulation and describes the findings as mixed.

What the Ingredient List Does and Doesn’t Tell You

A named ingredient list makes it easy to research each component, check for allergies and talk to your doctor. Without published amounts, though, you can’t compare doses with those used in studies. Studies on single ingredients also can’t predict what a combined formula will do.

Who should be careful

  • Anyone taking blood-pressure medication, nitrates or blood thinners
  • People with hormone-sensitive conditions (MedlinePlus flags maca and horny goat weed)
  • Anyone with liver concerns (NIH LiverTox notes rare reports with tongkat ali and ashwagandha)
  • Anyone under 18
